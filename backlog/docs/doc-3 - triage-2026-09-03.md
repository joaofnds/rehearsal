## Kaizen candidate

A pattern across three cards, worth naming once. Cards filed by a session at the
end of its own work carry counts measured at that moment, and the counts go stale
within days:

- ACT-26.7: "17 `console.log` sites" (14).
- ACT-27: "236 lines" (406).
- ACT-29: "over 1500" (210).

In every case the count is the card's evidence of size, and in every case the
acceptance criterion is written correctly against a behavior instead. Nothing
here is broken, but a reader prioritizing by the counts would misjudge all
three, and one of them by 170 lines in the wrong direction.

**Settled**, as decision-1. João's answer was to keep the counts but give the
reader a way to read them at their original value, the way a price is read
against inflation. A date alone does not do that: it says the number is old
without saying what today's is. The command that produced the number does,
because a reader re-runs it and compares two numbers of known provenance. All
four cards above are reproducible by a one-line command, so the rule costs the
writer nothing at the moment they have just run it. ACT-36 carries it into the
corpus, where it governs every board rather than this one.
